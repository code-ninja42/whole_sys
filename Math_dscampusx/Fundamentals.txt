Statistics Fundamentals — Interview Notes
1. Population vs Sample
Population

Definition:
A population is the complete group of people, objects, or observations that we want to study.

Example:
A university has 10,000 students, and we want to study the average height of all students.

Population = 10,000 students
Sample

Definition:
A sample is a smaller group selected from the population to represent the population in a study.

Example:

Instead of measuring all 10,000 students, we randomly select 500 students and measure their heights.

Population = 10,000 students
Sample = 500 students
Why do we use a sample?
Studying the entire population can be expensive.
It can take a lot of time.
A properly selected sample can help us estimate characteristics of the population.
Interview Answer

Population is the entire group of individuals or observations that we want to study, whereas a sample is a smaller subset selected from that population. We use samples because collecting data from the entire population can be expensive and time-consuming. A representative sample helps us make estimates about the population.

Key Difference
Population	Sample
Entire group	Smaller subset
Usually larger	Usually smaller
Complete group of interest	Selected from population
Example: 10,000 students	Example: 500 students
2. Descriptive vs Inferential Statistics

Statistics can mainly be divided into:

Descriptive Statistics
Inferential Statistics
2.1 Descriptive Statistics
Definition

Descriptive statistics is the process of collecting, organizing, summarizing, and presenting data in a meaningful way.

It describes the data that we already have.

Example

Suppose a teacher has marks of 50 students.

We can:

Calculate the average marks.
Find the highest and lowest marks.
Calculate standard deviation.
Create charts or graphs.

These are examples of descriptive statistics.

Common Techniques
Mean
Median
Mode
Range
Variance
Standard deviation
Tables
Charts
Graphs
Simple Meaning

Descriptive = Describe or summarize the data we have.

2.2 Inferential Statistics
Definition

Inferential statistics uses data from a sample to estimate or draw conclusions about a larger population, while considering uncertainty.

Example

A university has 10,000 students.

We randomly select 500 students and calculate their average height.

We can use the sample information to estimate the average height of all 10,000 students.

Common Techniques
Confidence intervals
Hypothesis testing
Z-test
t-test
Chi-square test
ANOVA
A/B testing
Simple Meaning

Inferential = Use sample data to make conclusions about a population.

Descriptive vs Inferential
Descriptive Statistics	Inferential Statistics
Summarizes data	Draws conclusions
Describes the data we have	Generalizes from sample to population
Does not necessarily generalize to a larger population	Uses sample data to make population-level conclusions
Mean, median, standard deviation	Hypothesis testing, confidence intervals
Example: Average marks of 50 students	Example: Estimate average marks of all university students
Easy Way to Remember

Descriptive → What does my data show?

Inferential → What can I conclude about the larger population?

Interview Answer

Descriptive statistics summarizes and presents the data we have using measures such as mean, median, and standard deviation. Inferential statistics uses sample data to make estimates or draw conclusions about a larger population. For example, calculating the average marks of a class is descriptive, while using a sample of students to estimate the average marks of an entire university is inferential.

3. Types of Data

Data is the information that we collect for analysis.

The two main types are:

Categorical Data
Numerical Data

Numerical data is further divided into:

Discrete Data
Continuous Data
3.1 Categorical Data
Definition

Categorical data represents groups, categories, or labels.

It describes what something is, rather than how much of it there is.

Examples
Gender → Male, Female, Other
Blood Group → A, B, AB, O
Department → IT, HR, Finance
City → Ahmedabad, Mumbai, Delhi
Customer Satisfaction → Dissatisfied, Neutral, Satisfied
Types of Categorical Data
Nominal

Categories have no natural order.

Examples:

Blood group
City
Department
Gender
Ordinal

Categories have a meaningful order or ranking.

Examples:

Low → Medium → High
Poor → Average → Good → Excellent
Dissatisfied → Neutral → Satisfied
Simple Meaning

Categorical data = Categories or labels.

3.2 Numerical Data
Definition

Numerical data represents quantities that can be counted or measured.

Arithmetic operations such as addition and averaging can be meaningful for numerical data.

Examples
Age → 22 years
Salary → ₹30,000
Height → 170 cm
Weight → 65 kg
Number of orders → 25

Numerical data is divided into:

Discrete Data
Continuous Data
3.3 Discrete Data
Definition

Discrete data consists of countable and separate values, usually whole numbers.

Examples
Number of students → 50
Number of orders → 20
Number of machine failures → 3
Number of employees → 100

We cannot normally have:

2.5 students
3.7 orders
10.4 employees
Simple Meaning

Discrete = Counted.

3.4 Continuous Data
Definition

Continuous data can take any value within a range, including decimal values.

It is usually obtained through measurement.

Examples
Height → 165.5 cm
Weight → 62.8 kg
Temperature → 36.7°C
Time → 3.45 seconds
Simple Meaning

Continuous = Measured.

Discrete vs Continuous
Discrete	Continuous
Countable	Measurable
Separate values	Can take any value within a range
Usually whole numbers	Can contain decimal values
Number of students	Height
Number of orders	Weight
Number of machine failures	Temperature
Interview Answer — Types of Data

Data is mainly classified into categorical and numerical data. Categorical data represents groups or labels, such as department or blood group. Numerical data represents quantities, such as age, salary, or height. Numerical data is further divided into discrete data, which is countable, and continuous data, which is measurable and can take decimal values.

Interview Answer — Discrete vs Continuous

Discrete data consists of countable, separate values, such as the number of students in a class. Continuous data can take any value within a range, such as a person's height or weight. Discrete data is usually obtained by counting, while continuous data is usually obtained by measuring.

4. Sampling Methods
Definition of Sampling

Sampling is the process of selecting a smaller group from a population to collect data and study its characteristics.

The three important sampling methods are:

Simple Random Sampling
Stratified Sampling
Cluster Sampling
4.1 Simple Random Sampling
Definition

Simple random sampling is a method in which every individual in the population has an equal chance of being selected.

Example

A university has 1,000 students.

We need a sample of 100 students.

We assign a unique number to every student and randomly select 100 numbers.

Every student has the same chance of being selected.

Advantages
Easy to understand.
Reduces selection bias when properly randomized.
Simple to analyze.
Limitation

It can be difficult when the population is very large or geographically distributed.

Simple Meaning

Simple Random = Randomly select individual people/items.

4.2 Stratified Sampling
Definition

Stratified sampling divides the population into subgroups called strata based on a shared characteristic.

Then, we randomly select individuals from each subgroup.

Example

A university has 1,000 students:

600 Undergraduate students
300 Postgraduate students
100 PhD students

We need a sample of 100 students.

Using proportional stratified sampling:

60 Undergraduate students
30 Postgraduate students
10 PhD students

Students are randomly selected from each group.

Advantages
Ensures representation from each subgroup.
Can improve the precision of estimates.
Allows comparison between subgroups.
Limitation

We need information about the population's subgroup membership.

Simple Meaning

Stratified = Divide into groups → randomly select from every group.

4.3 Cluster Sampling
Definition

Cluster sampling divides the population into natural groups called clusters.

We randomly select some clusters and collect data from the individuals in those selected clusters.

Example

A university has 50 classes with 40 students in each class.

We randomly select 5 classes and survey all students in those 5 classes.

Here:

Each class = Cluster
Selected classes = Sample
Students inside selected classes = Surveyed individuals
Advantages
Useful when the population is geographically distributed.
Can reduce cost and time.
Does not always require a complete list of every individual.
Limitation

Selected clusters may not represent the entire population well.

Simple Meaning

Cluster = Divide into groups → randomly select some whole groups.

5. Stratified vs Cluster Sampling
Stratified Sampling	Cluster Sampling
Population is divided into strata	Population is divided into clusters
Randomly select individuals from every group	Randomly select some groups
Every important subgroup is represented	Only selected clusters are included
Groups are based on a characteristic	Groups are usually natural existing groups
Example: Select students from every academic level	Example: Select certain classes and survey their students
Easy Way to Remember

Stratified:

Divide → Select from every group

Cluster:

Divide → Select some groups

6. Important Interview Questions
Q1. What is the difference between population and sample?

Population is the entire group of individuals or observations that we want to study, whereas a sample is a smaller subset selected from that population. We use samples because collecting data from the entire population can be expensive and time-consuming.

Q2. What is descriptive statistics?

Descriptive statistics summarizes and presents the data we have using measures such as mean, median, and standard deviation.

Q3. What is inferential statistics?

Inferential statistics uses sample data to make estimates or draw conclusions about a larger population while considering uncertainty.

Q4. What is the difference between descriptive and inferential statistics?

Descriptive statistics summarizes the data we have, whereas inferential statistics uses sample data to make conclusions or estimates about a larger population.

Q5. What are the main types of data?

The main types are categorical and numerical data. Numerical data is further divided into discrete and continuous data.

Q6. What is categorical data?

Categorical data represents groups or labels, such as department, blood group, or city.

Q7. What is numerical data?

Numerical data represents quantities that can be counted or measured, such as age, salary, height, or weight.

Q8. What is the difference between discrete and continuous data?

Discrete data is countable and consists of separate values, while continuous data is measurable and can take any value within a range, including decimal values.

Q9. What is simple random sampling?

Simple random sampling selects individuals randomly, where every individual in the population has an equal chance of being selected.

Q10. What is stratified sampling?

Stratified sampling divides the population into subgroups based on a shared characteristic and randomly selects individuals from each subgroup.

Q11. What is cluster sampling?

Cluster sampling divides the population into natural groups, randomly selects some of those groups, and collects data from the selected groups.

Q12. What is the difference between stratified and cluster sampling?

In stratified sampling, we select individuals from every subgroup, whereas in cluster sampling, we randomly select some groups and study the individuals within those selected groups.
